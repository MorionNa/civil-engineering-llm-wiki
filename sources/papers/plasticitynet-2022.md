# Li et al. (2022) — PlasticityNet

## Bibliographic Record

- **Title:** PlasticityNet: Learning to Simulate Metal, Sand, and Snow for Optimization Time Integration
- **Authors:** Xuan Li, Yadi Cao, Minchen Li, Yin Yang, Craig Schroeder, Chenfanfu Jiang
- **Venue:** NeurIPS 2022
- **Pages:** 14
- **Source file:** `NeurIPS-2022-plasticitynet-learning-to-simulate-metal-sand-and-snow-for-optimization-time-integration-Paper-Conference.pdf`

## Evidence Scope

Full-text review of the conference paper. The source covers the integrability obstacle of classical return mapping, a learned family of local elastoplastic potentials, fixed-point optimization time integration, a post-training stability regularizer, optional learning of volume-preserving return mappings, 2D/3D FEM and MPM experiments, and limitations.

## Evidence Map

- **pp. 1–2:** motivation, constitutive portability, relation to optimization integration and ECI.
- **pp. 3–4:** optimization time integration, return mapping and non-integrability; local potential construction and exact gradient correction.
- **pp. 5–6:** hardening state, fixed-point outer iteration, stability regularizer, learned volume-preserving return mapping and sampling.
- **pp. 7–9:** 2D timing/accuracy, sand, snow, metal, BFEMP coupling, representation ablations, stability ablation and 3D demonstrations.
- **p. 10:** conclusions, limitations and future work.
- **p. 14:** reproducibility checklist.

## Directly Supported Claims

1. PlasticityNet learns a local family of scalar elastoplastic potentials whose gradients approximate forces produced by elasticity combined with plastic return mapping.
2. A linear correction makes the learned potential gradient exact at the current expansion point.
3. A fixed-point loop repeatedly updates the expansion point and solves an optimization problem, allowing FEM and MPM to use optimization time integration with multiple plasticity models.
4. The paper demonstrates dry sand, hardening snow, metal with closed-form and learned return mappings, two-way BFEMP coupling, and 3D examples.
5. The method uses larger time steps than the explicit reference, but each implicit frame may be more expensive and the paper explicitly states that universal speed superiority is not its objective.

## Evidence Boundaries

- Fixed-point convergence is not guaranteed for arbitrary scenes.
- The post-training quadratic regularizer can introduce artificial viscosity.
- Training does not constrain the learned-energy Hessian, lower boundedness or convexity.
- A trained model can be rescaled for Young's modulus but must be retrained for different Poisson ratios or plastic parameters.
- Ground truth is generated by small-step explicit integration rather than experimental material data.
- The supplied main PDF does not provide a directly visible code repository URL.
